Metal Square Bar (Billet) Calculator

Square Profile Definition:

Side Dimension (A): The cross-sectional width and height (both are equal in square bars).

Length (L): Total longitudinal span of the square stock or billet.

Diagonal Dimension: Corner-to-corner point span = Side × √2 ≈ 1.4142 × A.

How This Calculator Works

Because a solid square bar has equal width and height, its cross-sectional area is the square of its side dimension:

Step 1 (Cross-Section Area): Area = Side × Side (A²) mm²
Step 2 (Volume cm³): (Side mm × Side mm × Length mm) ÷ 1,000
Step 3 (Single Piece Weight kg): (Volume cm³ × Density g/cm³) ÷ 1,000
Carbon Steel Quick Weight: Weight per meter (kg/m) ≈ A² × 0.00785 (where A is in mm)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Square Bar and Square Tube?

A square bar is 100% solid metal with no hollow core. A square tube (box pipe) is hollow with a specific wall thickness.

How do cold drawn and hot rolled square bars compare in weight?

Theoretical weight is identical based on alloy density. However, cold drawn bars feature sharp, precise corners, while hot-rolled bars feature slight corner radiusing (±1-2%).

Can I calculate rectangular flat bars here?

For unequal width and thickness flat bars (e.g., 50mm × 10mm), use our dedicated Metal Plate & Sheet Calculator.
