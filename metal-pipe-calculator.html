How This Calculator Works

The theoretical weight of a hollow metal pipe is computed by subtracting the inner cylinder volume from the outer cylinder volume, then multiplying by the material density:

Step 1 (Inner Diameter): ID = OD - (2 × WT)
Step 2 (Cross-Section Area): Area = π × (OD - WT) × WT mm²
Step 3 (Volume cm³): (Area mm² × Length mm) ÷ 1,000
Step 4 (Single Weight kg): (Volume cm³ × Density g/cm³) ÷ 1,000
Standard Carbon Steel Quick Formula: Weight (kg/m) = (OD - WT) × WT × 0.02466 (OD & WT in mm)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why does physical scale weight differ from theoretical weight?

Manufacturing rolling tolerances (±5% to 10%), mill scales, internal seam welds, and slight alloy composition variations affect actual physical scale weight.

What is the difference between Pipe and Tube?

Pipes are specified by Nominal Bore (internal fluid capacity) and Wall Schedule (e.g. Sch 40), whereas tubes are designated by precise Outer Diameter (OD) and Wall Thickness (WT).

How do I calculate per-meter or per-foot pipe weight?

Enter 1000 mm (1 meter) in Metric mode or 12 inches (1 foot) in Imperial mode to get instantaneous unit linear weight.
